Notes for every reader of these sets:

- **The firewall shortens rule names in the traffic log.** The log's Rule column holds 19 characters, so `isp-to-internet-gateway` prints as `isp-to-internet-gat`, `staff-to-users-gateway` as `staff-to-users-gate` and `operator-to-internal-gateway` as `operator-to-interna` (T/sessions/internet/traffic-log.txt:7; the verdicts name both forms, T/verdict.txt:10, :21, :32). `show session id` prints the full name (T/sessions/internet/session-detail.txt:31).
- **Each session's `traffic-log.txt` is the whole log of the last 15 minutes on port 443.** The population's own line is the one with its source port: internet T/sessions/internet/traffic-log.txt:6 to :9 (port 42936), staff T/sessions/enterprise/traffic-log.txt:6 to :9 (42937), operator T/sessions/operator/traffic-log.txt:6 to :9 (42938). The lines from ports 42946 to 42948 are the trace's warm-up connects (T/warmup/*.txt), not its sessions.
- **A refused connect leaves one log line a SYN.** The staff client's connect to the published address left four `deny-all-log` lines, the SYN and its three retransmits a second apart (A/zone-nat/traffic-log-443.txt:22 to :37); each break connect left five (B/record/check-break-tlog-syn.txt:6 to :25 for port 42312).
- **The SYN's MSS changes at the firewall.** The internet SYN left the ISP router with MSS 9460 and the staff SYN left ent-client with MSS 1460; both reached the bundle leg with MSS 1360 (T/captures/internet/1-isp-rtr-eth1.txt:3, 3-fw-ae1-leg2.txt:3; T/captures/enterprise/2-fw-ethernet1-4.txt:3, 3-fw-ae1-leg1.txt:3). The source, sequence number and IP id are kept and the TTL is one lower. Shade the source and sequence number, as the design says; do not say "only the destination changes" of the whole header: the MSS option and the checksum change too.
- **The API gateway's line for an internet caller is not in these sets.** The hold's chat requests are judged by their HTTP status (M/verdict.txt:10 to :12), and no set saves the API gateway's access line for `inetapp`; the audit's access-log check reads the staff's person caller only (A/verdict.txt:675). A sentence that the API gateway's line names the internet caller by its consumer group cites no line here.
- **What the session count after the stop shows depends on when it is read.** The clients closed their connections at about 19:24:50 (M/hold-*.jsonl, the `closed` events); the first read after the stop, at 19:25:01, still listed the staff's 8 sessions as ACTIVE (M/sessions-after-enterprise.txt:6 to :21; M/commands.txt:613), and every count read from 19:25:06 on was 0 (M/counts-after-*.txt:2; M/commands.txt:662 to :818). Quote the held and after-the-chat counts; the after-the-stop column is not a measure of a timeout.
- **The supported-sessions figure is this VM's.** `show session info` prints `Number of sessions supported: 1100000` (M/session-info-held.txt:4) for the lab's PA-VM. It is not a documented ceiling of any model; Chapter 4's documented ceilings stay the sizing reference.
